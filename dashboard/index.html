Farm economics

Reference deposit $1,000. Slice = 10% airdrop × implied value × deposit / TVL: the launch is assumed to airdrop 10% of supply to farmers, split pro-rata by TVL share. Net = slice − gas − forgone T-bill yield (5% a year over the hold). Gas is priced per action at mainnet-era rates — $3 to set up, $5 to deposit, $3 for a second use, $2 a week to hold, $3 to claim — so $22–$32 a plan; on a cheap L2 the same plan costs a few dollars, and a plan that nets positive at these rates nets more there. Break-even = total cost / slice: the launch odds at which the plan pays for itself; above 100% the plan never pays, whatever the odds. Verdict: FARM when net ≥ $25, THIN at $5–$25, MARGINAL at $0–$5, SKIP when negative. The deposit and drop-share filters recompute the slice, the net, the break-even and the verdict live; the seven steps are the $1,000 plan, with each step's own cost and time stated.

What the model does not claim

An implied value is the market cap a protocol would carry if it traded at the fitted curve — not a prediction that a token will launch, and not a promise that a farm pays. The leave-one-out miss is ×2.83: a single protocol's value is uncertain by a factor of three either way, and the rank ranges say the same. 52 of 196 rows are FARM at $1,000; most of the rest are SKIP because a $1,000 slice of a big protocol is a few dollars and gas eats it; the $10,000 deposit filter shows the same plans at a size where more of them pay.
