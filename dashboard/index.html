Farm economics

Reference deposit $1,000. Slice = 10% airdrop × implied value × deposit / TVL. Net = slice − gas − forgone T-bill yield (5% a year over the hold). Break-even = total cost / slice: the launch odds at which the plan pays for itself; above 100% it never pays. Verdict: FARM when net ≥ $25, THIN at $5–$25, MARGINAL at $0–$5, SKIP when negative. The deposit and drop-share filters recompute the slice, the net, the break-even and the verdict live; the seven steps are the $1,000 plan, with each step's own cost and time stated. Gas is priced at mainnet-era rates ($3 to set up, $5 to deposit, $3 for a second use, $2 a week, $3 to claim) — on an L2 the same plan only costs less, so this is the conservative figure, not a low one.

What the model does not claim

An implied value is the market cap a protocol would carry if it traded at the fitted curve — not a prediction that a token will launch, and not a promise that a farm pays. The leave-one-out miss is ×3.64: a single protocol's value is uncertain by a factor of four either way, and the rank ranges say the same. Most rows are SKIP or THIN at $1,000 because a $1,000 slice of a big protocol is a few dollars and gas eats it; the $10,000 deposit filter shows the same plans at a size where they start to pay.
